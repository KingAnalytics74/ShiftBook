      Holiday in your kind of work

      Tap your job. Everyone gets at least 5.6 weeks of paid holiday a year, whatever their contract says.

      Care and support work
      • Changing shifts? Choose "My hours change". You earn 12.07% of every hour you work, so 10 hours earns about 1.2 hours of holiday.
      • Sleep-ins: time asleep usually isn't counted as working time. If your payslip turns sleep-ins into hours (for example £40 ÷ £13 = 3.08 hours), they may count. Check your payslip or ask HR, then set it above.
      • Waking nights are work, so every paid hour counts.
      • Travel between visits in home care is working time and should count too.
      • Holiday pay should include regular overtime and shift enhancements, not just your basic rate.
      NHS and hospitals
      • Agenda for Change staff usually get 27 days plus 8 bank holidays, rising to 29 days after 5 years and 33 after 10 (full-time). Choose "Same days every week" and type your contract days.
      • Bank staff usually build up 12.07% of hours worked, or get it added to each payslip. Choose "My hours change" or "Holiday pay on every payslip".
      • Part-time staff get the same pro-rata, worked out in hours.
      Factory, warehouse and production
      • Fixed rotas (like 4 on, 4 off) count as regular hours even if the days move. Use "Same days every week" with your average days a week, for example 3.5.
      • Varying shifts or overtime-only work: choose "My hours change".
      • Shutdowns: your employer can make you take holiday, for example at Christmas, if they tell you at least twice as many days before as the holiday lasts.
      • Night and shift premiums that you're paid regularly should be in your holiday pay.
      Retail, hospitality and leisure
      • Zero-hours or casual? Choose "My hours change". Holiday builds up from your first shift.
      • Many employers pay it as rolled-up holiday pay: a separate "holiday pay" line on every payslip. If yours does, choose "Holiday pay on every payslip". You still have the right to take the time off, unpaid.
      • Regular overtime and commission should be included in holiday pay.
      Agency and temporary work
      • You build up holiday from day one. The agency pays it, not the place you work at.
      • After 12 weeks in the same job you're entitled to the same holiday as staff employed directly there.
      • If you leave or the job ends, you must be paid for holiday you've built up but not taken.
      Delivery, driving, security and cleaning
      • Paid hours count, including waiting time when you have to stay on site or with the vehicle.
      • Two jobs? You build up holiday separately in each one.
      • Self-employed couriers and drivers don't get holiday pay, unless they're really workers. If an app or company controls your shifts and pay, you may be a worker. Check with ACAS.
      School and term-time work
      • If you only work during term, you're a part-year worker. You build up 12.07% of hours you work, so choose "My hours change".
      • Your employer can't give you less than 5.6 weeks' worth just because you don't work all year.

      Your holiday rights in plain English

      Booking holiday

      Give at least twice as many days' notice as the holiday you want: 2 days off needs 4 days' notice, unless your contract says otherwise. Your employer can say no, but must tell you at least as many days before as the holiday you asked for.

      Bank holidays

      There's no legal right to a paid day off on a bank holiday. Many employers count bank holidays as part of your 5.6 weeks. If you work one, you get the day off another time, or extra pay only if your contract says so.

      Carrying holiday over

      Most people can carry up to 8 days (1.6 weeks) into the next year if their employer agrees. If you couldn't take holiday because you were off sick, on maternity or family leave, or your employer stopped you, you can usually carry more over.

      Sick on holiday

      If you're ill during your holiday, tell your employer straight away. You can usually take those days as sick leave and have the holiday back later.

      Leaving your job

      You must be paid for holiday you've built up but not taken. Your employer can take back pay for holiday you took but hadn't built up yet only if your contract allows it.

      What holiday pay should include

      For at least 4 weeks of your holiday, pay should include regular overtime, commission and shift enhancements, worked out as the average of the last 52 weeks you were paid. Weeks you weren't paid don't count.

      These are the legal minimums in England, Scotland and Wales. Northern Ireland has its own rules, so check nidirect. Your contract can give you more, never less. Shiftbuck's figures are estimates, not legal advice. For help, contact ACAS or see GOV.UK.

      Privacy

      Last updated 1 November 2026

      In short

      Shiftbuck keeps your shift, pay and money details so it can work out your pay. We don't sell your data, show ads, track you with cookies or share anything with your employer.

      Who we are

      Shiftbuck is made by WesByte Systems, which is responsible for this app. Contact us by messaging the Shiftbuck team in the app (Community → Messages).

      What Shiftbuck keeps

      • What you enter in setup: your first name, role, hourly and sleep-in rates, pay period start day and usual shift times.
      • Your profile: your council area, your employer (optional) and your community nickname. Other carers only ever see your nickname and council area. Your employer never sees any of it.
      • How you sign in: your mobile number or email address and, if you choose one, a password. If you continue with Google, Google tells us your email address and name, and nothing else. Your password is only ever kept scrambled (hashed) by Supabase, so nobody at Shiftbuck can read it.
      • Your shifts: dates, start and finish times, shift type, hours, breaks, sleep-ins, any shift rate, notes, and the workplace labels you choose to add.
      • Payslip figures you type in, your tax settings, your spending plan and spending amounts.
      • In the community: your public profile (nickname and area only), your posts, and messages you send. Messages to the Shiftbuck team are read only by you and the team.

      Shiftbuck does not ask for your bank details, National Insurance number, address or date of birth, and it isn't connected to your bank.

      The people you support

      Shiftbuck never needs any details about the people you support. Please don't add their names, addresses, health details or other personal information anywhere in Shiftbuck, including notes.

      Where your data is kept and who can see it

      When you sign in, your data is kept in Shiftbuck's database, run on Supabase. It's tied to your account and locked so that only you can read or change it. Supabase stores and processes it for us under its own privacy policy.

      In demo mode, nothing is sent anywhere: your data stays in this browser on this device.

      Nobody else using Shiftbuck can see your data: not other carers and not your employer. The only exceptions are what you choose to share: your posts and profile in the community, private messages (only you and the carer you're talking to can read them, unless one of you reports a message to the moderators), and messages to the Shiftbuck team.

      Cookies and storage on your device

      Google Analytics, only if you say yes. When you first visit, Shiftbuck asks whether we can count visits with Google Analytics. If you tap Accept, Google sets cookies that tell us how many people visit, which pages they open and which of our ads brought them. We don't send Google your shifts, pay, payslips, messages, name, email or phone number, and Google's advertising features are turned off. If you tap No thanks, nothing is sent to Google. You can change your mind any time with Cookie settings at the bottom of every screen. Google processes this under its own privacy policy.

      Microsoft Clarity, only if you say yes. The same Accept also turns on Microsoft Clarity, which records how people move around the app (taps, clicks and scrolling) so we can see where it's confusing. All text on screen is hidden in these recordings, so Microsoft never sees your pay, payslips, messages, name or anything you type, and its advertising use is turned off. Microsoft processes this under its own privacy statement. No thanks, or changing your mind in Cookie settings, turns it off too.

      Shiftbuck doesn't use cookies for advertising. It keeps a few things in your browser: which tab you last had open, so the app opens where you left off. When you're signed in it also keeps your sign-in session, so you stay signed in; in demo mode it keeps all your Shiftbuck data in this browser. It also remembers your cookie choice. Fonts and code come from Shiftbuck's own site. Apart from Google Analytics and Microsoft Clarity when you've said yes, the only other service Shiftbuck talks to is Supabase, which stores your data.

      Why we use it

      Only to give you the app: working out your pay, take-home, rest check, charts and money plan. Your information isn't used for anything else.

      Your rights

      • See and download everything: Timesheet → Back up all data.
      • Correct anything: edit a shift, payslip or setting at any time.
      • Delete a single shift from its edit screen, or everything with the button below.

      You also have rights under UK data protection law. If you're unhappy with how your data is handled, contact us, or complain to the Information Commissioner's Office.

      How long it's kept

      Until you delete it. If this version of Shiftbuck closes, you'll be told in advance so you can download your data.

      Questions and answers

      What is Shiftbuck?
      A shift log for care and support workers. Log each shift, including sleep-ins and breaks, and Shiftbuck shows your gross pay, an estimate of your take-home, a check against your payslip, a rest warning and a plan for your money.
      How is my pay worked out?
      For each shift: (paid hours − unpaid break) × your hourly rate, plus your sleep-in rate if it was a sleep-in. If a shift has its own rate, such as a higher rate for nights or weekends, that rate is used instead. Paid hours are worked out from the start and finish times, including shifts that run past midnight. Your rates and shift types are in Settings on the Timesheet tab.
      Is my take-home exact?
      It's an estimate using 2026/27 tax, National Insurance and pension rules for England, Wales and Northern Ireland. Your payslip is the final word. Add your payslip on the Money tab and Shiftbuck compares it line by line.
      My payslip doesn't match. What should I do?
      Check the hours and sleep-ins logged for that pay period first. If the payslip shows no sleep-in pay but you did sleep-ins, or fewer hours than you worked, ask your payroll team. The payslip check shows exactly which line is different.
      Why is my Rest check red?
      Red means you're not getting enough rest: 7 or more days in a row without a day off, more than 48 hours in 7 days, or a day shift, sleep-in and another day shift back to back. Amber means watch it, for example less than 11 hours off between the end of one shift and the start of the next. It's a guide based on UK working time rules, not legal advice.
      What does 'unpaid breaks worth' mean?
      The hours of unpaid breaks you took, multiplied by your hourly rate. It shows the time you give at work without pay.
      How do I change or delete a shift?
      Tap the day on the calendar or its row on the Timesheet tab. Change the details and tap Save changes, or tap Delete. If you only worked part of a shift, change the hours instead of deleting it.
      How do I send my timesheet to my manager?
      Timesheet tab → Download CSV. The file includes your name, the pay period, every shift and the totals. It opens in Excel or Google Sheets.
      Can my employer see my data?
      No. Your data is private to your account. Your employer, your manager and other carers can't see it. Only what you choose to send, such as a downloaded timesheet, leaves the app.
      I forgot my password. What do I do?
      On the sign-in screen, tap Sign in with email and password, then Forgot your password? We'll email you a link to choose a new one. It works for one hour. You can also sign in with a code sent to your email instead. If you signed up with an email code, the same link lets you add a password.
      Can I use Shiftbuck on more than one device?
      Yes. Sign in with the same phone number, email or password on your phone or computer and you'll see the same data. In demo mode, data stays on one device.
      Should I add the people I support?
      No. Shiftbuck only needs your shifts, not who they were with. If you work in more than one place, add a short workplace name instead, like "Site 1" or "Day centre". Never a person's name or a house address.
      Does it work for weekly pay, Scotland or two jobs?
      Not yet. Shiftbuck currently supports monthly pay periods starting on any day you choose, one hourly and one sleep-in rate, and tax rules for England, Wales and Northern Ireland. These are on the roadmap.
